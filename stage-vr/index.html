How the windows work

Each window is a hole showing what the rear camera can actually see behind it, worked out from where you put the window and where your head is pointed. Both share the one camera, so they show different parts of the room, not the same picture twice.

Where the camera cannot reach, the window is black. A phone lens sees about 65 degrees across, so the keys window is usually dark while you are looking out at the house, and fills in as you tilt your head down towards it. That is the lens telling the truth, not a fault.

One camera feeding two eyes reads flat and runs about a tenth of a second behind. Fine for glancing, wrong for reaching. If the camera is unavailable the keys window falls back to a drawn keybed, which is a placeholder and not your keys; trim the bottom edge of the cardboard and look down at your real keyboard through the gap.

Changing things once you are inside

Everything lives behind one small menu tab parked off to your left. Look at it for a moment to open it, or press the viewer button if yours has one. Nothing else answers to your gaze, so you can watch your own hands through the piano window for as long as you like and nothing will move. The menu folds itself away if you stop using it.

Inside: piano, music window and sound, never more than four choices on screen. Pick move it to pick a window up; it then follows your head, so look where you want it and hold still for a moment to set it down. Once placed, it stays put in the room while you look around.

On iPhone, if the view will not follow your head: Settings, Apps, Safari, turn on Motion & Orientation Access, then reload.
